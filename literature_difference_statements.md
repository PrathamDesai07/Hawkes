# "Difference from" statements and related-work draft (Problem 22)

Date: 25 Oct 2026. Companion to `literature_unverified_items.md` (Problem 23), `literature_crosscheck.md`, `literature_search_report.md`, `novelty_reassessment.md`.

**How to read this.** Each paragraph states what the cited work does and how ours differs, *at the access level stated*. Access levels: **FT** full text read; **PT** part of the full text read; **ABS** abstract/metadata only; **SC** scanned PDF obtained but not machine-readable. A claim that a paper "does not" treat something means: not in the parts I read. Nothing here is a statement about what the paper does not contain beyond those parts. Herrera-Marín I and II are unrefereed preprints. Our own results are drafts (tags in each note), none refereed.

Our results referred to below (contribution in brackets): the log-loss regret (d/2) ln T of the Fisher-preconditioned online Newton with exact F-norm projection (Problems 5–7, C1–C2); the information dichotomy and the μ–n ridge (Problems 10–12, C4); the tracking bound (Problems 13–14, C5); and, for misspecification (C6), Problem 16 (existence and uniqueness of the exact KL pseudo-truth θ†, first-order conditions, rate matching μ† = (1 − n†)Λ*, criterion for the constraint n ≤ 1 − ε to bind, explicit strong convexity constant, sandwich variance, active-constraint limit law), Problem 17 (almost-sure convergence of the online estimator to θ†, limit laws, active constraint), Problem 18 (bias of n(θ†) versus K).

## A. The core papers (read order 1–6)

**1. Herrera-Marín, arXiv 2609.38268, "Learning the exogenous rate but not the distance to criticality…" [FT].**
*What it does.* A kernel-agnostic two-point bound KL(P₀ᵀ‖P₁ᵀ) ≤ δ²(1+c)I_T/(ρ₀ρ₁), I_T = Tμ(1 − ρ₀) (Thm 2.2, Cor 2.3, covering sums of exponentials from an empty start with shared μ and shape), the Fisher information of (log μ, log(1 − ρ)) (Thm 4.1), and a half-open Feller-type dichotomy in the light-tailed Jaisson–Rosenbaum regime (Remark 4.8: baseline information diverges for CIR shape κ ≤ 1; the converse is stated as unproved). Its main theorems concern the heavy-tailed rough regime, where the exogenous rate is learned but the margin is not.
*Difference.* We use Thm 2.2 as a cited lemma (I re-derived it and tested it, 12/12 configurations) and do not claim a new two-point bound. We (i) prove the bounded half of the dichotomy with rates, for K = 1 and for sums of exponentials, in both observation models; (ii) show that in the light-tailed regime the unlearnable direction is the μ–n ridge (the opposite asymmetry to the heavy-tailed case); (iii) study streaming estimators and log-loss regret, which the preprint does not treat. The statements in (i)–(ii) are my own drafts and have been verified only to the extent in `verification_thmA_E_F.md`.

**2. Herrera-Marín, arXiv 2609.38267, "Minimax and adaptive inference for branching ratios under long memory" [PT: abstract, Sec. 5, Sec. 6.2].**
*What it does.* Minimax rates in a long-memory model with fixed ρ; Section 5 fits K exponentials to a long-memory kernel and reports, numerically, that the quadratic-surrogate pseudo-true branching ratio is predicted to ≈ 10 % and that the criterion develops an "almost flat direction" from K ≈ 5 (which the author calls a property of the experiment).
*Difference.* (a) We define the pseudo-truth through the exact Hawkes KL (the limit of the likelihood), prove it exists and is unique on Θ_ε and on the cone, characterise it by KKT conditions and the rate-matching identity μ† = (1 − n†)Λ*, and prove that n(θ†) < 1 holds without imposing it (Problem 16). (b) We prove that the online estimator converges to it, including when the stability constraint binds (Problem 17). (c) For a power-law truth on a geometric grid we give the bias law n* − n(θ†) ≈ κ(η)·n*(τβ₁)^η/Γ(1+η), geometric in K at rate η ln r (Problem 18, a numerically supported conjecture), and compute the pseudo-truth deterministically as a weighted L² pole approximation with the Hawkes spectrum as weight. The "flat direction" is, in our terms, the λ_min(Hessian) decay with K quantified in Prop. 16.6 (the certified constant is 6 % to 0.3 % of the true smallest Hessian eigenvalue for K = 1…4 at n* = 0.8 and K = 3 at n* = 0.95). Theorem 3.1 and its proof are **not** checked; the surrogate part of Sec. 5 may overlap with Prop. 18.2 (see Rong–Nair below).

**3. Hall and Willett, "Tracking Dynamic Point Processes on Networks", IEEE Trans. IT 2016, arXiv 1409.0031 [PT: Thm 1 statement, Appendix C].**
*What it does.* Mirror descent on θ = log(δλ) to track an intensity vector with a known (or learned) dynamical model Φ_t; tracking regret bound C(1 + Σ‖λ_{t+1} − Φ_t(λ_t)‖²)√T with C depending on the bounds λ_min, λ_max.
*Difference.* The tracked object is the intensity vector in a Poisson-bin model, not the parameter of an exponential-dictionary Hawkes process; the regret is √T, not logarithmic, and does not expose the branching ratio. We estimate (μ, α) with logarithmic regret in the Hawkes likelihood and show the dependence on ε = 1 − n (Problems 5–7), and for drift we bound the Fisher-norm tracking error of a constant-gain recursion, η σ²(ε) + Δ²/η² + J D²/(ηT) (Problems 13–14). Proof of their Thm 1 not checked.

**4–5. Yang, Etesami, He and Kiyavash, "Online Learning for Multivariate Hawkes Processes", NeurIPS 2017; and "Nonparametric Hawkes Processes: Online Estimation and Generalization Bounds", arXiv 1801.08273 [FT for the arXiv version, assumptions, eq. (8)–(9), Thm 2, Cor 1; NeurIPS version not read].**
*What it does.* A nonparametric online estimator in an RKHS with Tikhonov regularisation, discretisation and truncation; O(log T) regret (Thm 2) against the *regularised, discretised, truncated* loss, plus a discretisation/truncation term (Cor 1); assumes bounded increments N(t) − N(t − z) ≤ κ_z, μ ∈ [μ_min, μ_max]. The branching ratio does not appear in the theorems.
*Difference.* The comparator in our regret is the unregularised log-likelihood; the state is exact and of size O(K) without truncation window; the constants are tracked as functions of ε. The regularisation they need (to make the loss strongly convex) is unnecessary in our analysis, which uses the Fisher-preconditioned Newton step and the fact that E⟨g, v⟩ = E vᵀΔF v (Prop. 1 of `problem05_online_newton.md`); the loss is not exp-concave (Lemma `rankone`), so the standard ONS route is unavailable and Yang et al. avoid it by changing the objective. Under misspecification, our estimator converges to the exact KL pseudo-truth rather than to a regularised optimum (Problem 17). My inference that their κ_z scales like μz/ε near criticality is mine, not a statement of the paper.

**6. Piggott and Solo, "Non-Negative Online Estimation for Hawkes Process Networks", ICASSP 2018 [FT, read 25 Oct 2026]; Pasha and Solo, "Adaptive Estimation of a Vector Point Process Model", CDC 2019 [FT, read 25 Oct 2026].**
*What they do.* Piggott–Solo: multivariate **linear** Hawkes with the kernel in a Laguerre (Erlang) basis sharing one decay constant; fixed-gain exponentially weighted **least squares** by a one-step non-negative-matrix-factorisation multiplicative update; simulations; non-negativity enforced, stability not ("extensions enforcing the stability constraint … will be pursued elsewhere"); no theorem. Pasha–Solo: a **log-linear** (nonlinear) Hawkes model, so no sign constraints; fixed-gain instantaneous steepest ascent on the point-process likelihood; a heuristic averaging analysis under stationarity and well-specification (locally stable if E[φφᵀλ] is positive definite, which they do not prove, and a "hovering theorem" they do not prove); genomic data.
*Difference.* Neither paper contains a regret bound, a tracking bound, an ε-dependence, a misspecification analysis or a stability-constrained linear model. We give a decreasing-gain Newton with log-loss regret, a constant-gain tracking bound with explicit ε-dependence (Problems 13–14), and convergence to the pseudo-truth with the stability face active. The averaging-and-ergodic-theorem route in Pasha–Solo (mean field g(θ), linearisation R) is the same mean-field structure as in our Theorem 17A, proved there only heuristically and for a different model. See `refpapers_conclusions.md`.

## B. Papers that bear on Problems 16–18 specifically (new in this pass)

**7. Rong and Nair, "Hawkes Identification with a Prescribed Causal Basis: Closed-Form Estimators and Asymptotics", arXiv 2602.20795 [PT: first ≈ 200 of 213 thousand characters of the HTML].**
*What it does.* Univariate linear Hawkes process; kernel approximated by a linear combination of prescribed unit-mass causal kernels (Hawkes–Laguerre as main example); *batch* least-squares contrast on (0, T]; closed form α̂ = R_T⁻¹ s_T, ĉ = Λ̂_T − χ̂_Tᵀα̂ (Thm 7); consistency and CLT under correct specification (Thm 17) and under misspecification with a pseudo-true parameter α* = R*⁻¹R*^{(1,0)}, Γ* = 𝟙ᵀα*, c* = Λ − μᵀα* (Section 5.3, Thm 15, Thm 21), via a martingale functional CLT with no mixing assumption; numerics for the Hawkes–Laguerre basis P = 1…5 against a *sum of exponentials* truth (Γ* = 0.67, 0.64, 0.70, 0.69, 0.70 for true 0.7, non-monotone, no rate). Requires Γ < 1; constants blow up as Γ → 1. Not covered in the parts I read: likelihood/KL pseudo-truth, online estimation, positivity/stability constraints (Γ* < 1, c* > 0 not proved), power-law truths, a rate in P.
*Difference.* (i) Their pseudo-truth is the least-squares one (our Q); ours is the exact KL (the limit of the likelihood), which is what the online algorithm of Problems 5–7 targets. Numerically the two differ by 0.1–0.3 % in θ in my examples, so the advantage is structural. (ii) They note Γ* = 𝟙ᵀα* but do not prove it is < 1; we prove n(θ†) < 1 for the KL pseudo-truth from rate matching (Cor. 2), and characterise when the constraint n ≤ 1 − ε binds and what happens then (Theorem 16.10, 17C). (iii) Online estimator with almost-sure convergence and limit laws (Problem 17). (iv) For a power-law truth we give a bias law in K (Problem 18). **Overlap resolved (checked in their PDF, 25 Oct 2026):** their Lemma 4(b) is the Hawkes Bartlett spectrum Λ/|1 − φ̂₀(ω)|², their Lemma 13 gives R* and R*^(1,0) as the double integrals of q C φ₀ and their Parseval form with that spectrum as weight, and Theorem 15 gives α* = R*⁻¹R*^(1,0), c* = Λ − μᵀα* = Λ(1 − Γ*). So the frequency-domain weighted-L² form of the least-squares pseudo-truth (my Prop. 18.2) and the surrogate rate matching (my Cor. 2(iv)) are **known and are not claimed**. What they do not have: the KL pseudo-truth, the proof that Γ* < 1 (their c* can be negative; for the KL pseudo-truth n† < 1 follows from rate matching because μ > 0 is part of the domain), the binding criterion for n ≤ 1 − ε, any online estimator, any bias law in the number of basis functions for a power-law truth. **Also checked:** their CLT without mixing (Lemmas 18–20, Theorem 21) rests on a martingale representation of the *true intensity* λ₀(t) − Λ = (ψ ⋆ dM)(t) through the Hawkes resolvent, which works because the least-squares score is bilinear in path functionals of N; the KL score contains the nonlinear factor 1/λ†, so this route does **not** transfer, and the CLT under mixing for Prop. 16.7 / Theorem 16.10 remains.

**8. Wei, Zhu, Zhang and Xie, "Goodness-of-Fit Test for Mismatched Self-Exciting Processes", AISTATS 2021, arXiv 2006.09439 [PT: first ≈ 100 000 of 136 000 characters of the ar5iv text].**
*What it does.* QMLE for self-exciting processes with a piecewise-constant triggering function (truncated on [0, T₀]); the target θ₀ = argmax E[ℓ] is interpreted as the KL projection onto the approximating class; global identifiability from concavity of the expected log-likelihood (Prop. 2); almost-sure convergence of the QMLE (Lemma 1(i)); sandwich covariance Σ⁻¹ = B⁻¹AB⁻¹ (Lemma 1(ii)); a goodness-of-fit test statistic. Batch; assumes θ₀ interior; near-critical not analysed.
*Difference.* The *generic* statements "KL pseudo-truth exists and is unique by concavity; QMLE converges to it; sandwich variance" are anticipated by this paper for their model class, and Problem 16 should not be presented as the first such statement. What is not in it, in the parts read: the exponential dictionary with the stability set {μ ≥ μ_min, α ≥ 0, n ≤ 1 − ε}, the rate-matching identity and its consequences, the criterion for the constraint to bind and the active-constraint limit law, the explicit strong-convexity constant, the online estimator, and the near-critical context. **Overlap-risk update: Medium for Problem 16.**

**9. Spreij, "Recursive parameter estimation for counting processes with linear intensity", Stochastics 18 (1986) 277–312; "Recursive approximate maximum likelihood estimation for a class of counting process models", J. Multivariate Anal. 39 (1991) 236–245 [FT, both read 25 Oct 2026; equations read from page images].**
*What they do.* Intensity λ_t = θᵀφ_t with φ observed and predictable, true θ₀ in the model. The AML recursion dx = Qφ/(φᵀθ̂)(dn − φᵀx dt), Q⁻¹ = Q₀⁻¹ + ∫φφᵀ/(φᵀθ̂)ds, with a safeguard replacing x by a known lower bound ε (1986) or by the box [ε, 1/ε]^d (1991). A.s. consistency (1986 Thm 3.2: Φᵀθ₀ → ∞ and a normalised Fisher matrix with liminf > 0; 1991 Thm 3: Lai–Wei growth of the extreme eigenvalues of Q⁻¹ only), by the stochastic Lyapunov function x̃ᵀQ⁻¹x̃; asymptotic normality with the Cramér–Rao covariance (1986 Thm 5.4, 1991 Thm 7). No Hawkes example, no misspecification, no regret, no near-critical analysis, no ε-dependence.
*Difference.* **The online recursion of Problems 5 and 17 is Spreij's AML recursion applied to the Hawkes regressor** (in event-indexed form with the gap integral done exactly), and the well-specified a.s. convergence and Cramér–Rao CLT are his theorems: we cite them and do not claim them. What is ours: the log-loss regret (d/2) ln T with explicit ε-dependence; the exact F-norm convex projection onto Θ_ε in place of a coordinate safeguard (his needs every component of θ₀ strictly inside a known box); the observed-information variant; and the misspecified analysis: convergence to the KL pseudo-truth, the drift matrix I_F⁻¹H ≠ I, the Lyapunov-equation limit law, the active stability face. His martingale argument cannot handle the non-martingale term of the misspecified score, which is why we need the ergodic/Poisson-equation route. See `refpapers_conclusions.md`.

## D. Draft related-work paragraphs

*Online estimation of Hawkes processes.* Online estimators for Hawkes processes follow three lines. Hall and Willett (2016) track intensity vectors by mirror descent with √T tracking regret under a known dynamical model; Yang et al. (2017, 2018) obtain O(log T) regret for a regularised, discretised and truncated nonparametric loss; Piggott and Solo (2018, least squares on a Laguerre basis, non-negativity preserved) and Pasha and Solo (2019, a log-linear model) propose fixed-gain recursions evaluated by simulation, without theorems. Recursive approximate maximum likelihood for a counting-process intensity linear in the parameter is due to Spreij (1986, 1991), who proves almost-sure consistency and the Cramér–Rao asymptotic normality in the well-specified case by a stochastic Lyapunov argument; the Fisher-preconditioned Newton recursion we analyse is his recursion applied to the Hawkes regressor. None of these makes the dependence on the distance to criticality explicit, bounds the log-loss regret, or treats a misspecified kernel; we do these, and we treat the stability constraint as an active convex constraint.

*Near-critical information.* Herrera-Marín (2026a) proves a kernel-agnostic two-point bound and a Feller-type dichotomy in the heavy-tailed and light-tailed regimes, leaving the bounded half of the light-tailed dichotomy open; the CIR-type scaling limits are due to Jaisson and Rosenbaum (2015). We prove the bounded half with rates, extend it to sums of exponentials, and identify the μ–n ridge as the unlearnable direction.

*Misspecification.* Quasi-maximum-likelihood theory for self-exciting processes (Wei et al., 2021) gives the KL pseudo-truth, its identifiability by concavity and the sandwich covariance for a piecewise-constant triggering function; Rong and Nair (2026) give closed-form least-squares estimators on a prescribed basis with pseudo-true parameters, a CLT without mixing, and numerical pseudo-true values for a sum-of-exponentials truth. Herrera-Marín (2026b) reports numerically a flat direction in the quadratic criterion for fits of K exponentials to a long-memory kernel. We characterise the exact KL pseudo-truth of the exponential dictionary (rate matching; when the stability constraint binds), prove that the online Newton estimator converges to it with and without an active constraint, and give a bias law in K for a power-law truth.

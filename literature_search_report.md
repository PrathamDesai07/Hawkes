## 2. CIR drift estimation

- [FT] The classical LAN/LAQ/LAMN theory is for **fixed** regimes: subcritical b₀ > 0 (ergodic), critical b₀ = 0, supercritical b₀ < 0 (the paper cites Overbeck 1998 for continuous observation). Their model is dX = (a − bX)dt + √(2σX) dB with a ≥ σ (Feller). I searched the text for "nearly", "near-critical", "local to": **no hit**. A local-to-critical experiment (b = c/T) is not treated there.
- [FT] **Thm 2.1 (subcritical):** LAN at rate √(nΔ_n) for both parameters with Fisher information I(a₀,b₀) = (1/2σ)[[b₀/(a₀−σ), −1], [−1, a₀/b₀]]. So the information about the drift constant a is finite exactly when a₀ > σ (Feller) and is proportional to b₀.
- [FT] **Cor 2.3(i) (critical, b = 0 known):** LAN for a at rate √log(nΔ_n) with I(a₀) = 1/(2σ(a₀−σ)): the baseline is estimable but only at a logarithmic rate. Supercritical: the LAMN result is for b alone with a known.
- [FT] Dehtiar et al.: the MLE of (a,b) from continuous observation is stated to be well defined only when 2a > σ²; the likelihood contains ∫dr/r and ∫dt/r (their eq. (9): â_T = (∫r dt·∫dr/r − T(r_T − r₀))/(∫r dt·∫dt/r − T²)), which need the path to stay off zero; Prop 1: for 2a > σ² the process is positive (Feller's test), ergodic, with Gamma stationary law of shape 2a/σ². (That the integrals diverge for 2a ≤ σ² is my inference, not a statement I read in the paper.) They also give another estimator defined for all positive parameters, not examined.

**Cross-checks I computed (`lit_check_cir_fisher.py`).** Under the map (a,b) = (βμ, βε), 2σ = β² from the diffusion approximation dλ ≈ (βμ − βελ)dt + β√λ dB, my exact K=1 Hawkes Fisher matrix converges to the matrix of Thm 2.1: relative differences (I_aa, I_ab, I_bb) at ε = 0.1, 0.01, 0.001 for μ = β = 1 are (0.225, 0.061, 0.007), (0.051, 0.009, 9×10⁻⁵), (0.008, 0.001, 10⁻⁶), all ∝ ε; for μ = 0.8 (nearer the threshold) (0.353, 0.081, 0.009), (0.123, 0.013, 1.4×10⁻⁴), (0.035, 0.002, 1.6×10⁻⁶). The critical-case coefficient β²·I(a) = 2/(β(2μ−β)) equals the cold-start log T coefficient conjectured in `problem10_rigorous.md` §4 (2.0000 and 3.3333 for μ = 1, 0.8). The MLE denominator ∫r·∫(1/r) − T² is the empirical analogue of my det I_T = T²(E[λ]E[1/λ] − 1)/α².

**Reading.** The bounded-versus-divergent information structure (finite iff a > σ, i.e. k > 1; a only √log T-estimable in the critical case) is classical for the CIR limit experiment. What I did not find: any statement for the local-to-critical CIR (b = c/T) or any Hawkes prelimit transfer. (Applying Thm 2.1 at b = c/T gives total information c/(2σ(a−σ)) for a, bounded; that is an extrapolation of a fixed-b theorem, not a result in the paper.)

## 3. Discrete analogues

- [FT] **Kevei 2011.** Nearly critical Galton–Watson with immigration. If G″_n(1)/(1−ρ_n) → 0 (offspring variance vanishing fast) the limit of X_n is Poisson/compound Poisson (the INAR(1)-type case of Györfi–Ispány–Pap–Varga). If G″_n(1) is of the order of 1−ρ_n (and immigration of that order), the limit is **negative binomial**: the discrete analogue of the Gamma law whose shape is the ratio of immigration to offspring variance. This is about the distribution, not estimation.
- [FT] **Ma 2009**, introduction: CLSE of the offspring mean is consistent and asymptotically normal in the non-critical case; in the critical or nearly critical case with positive limiting offspring variance, Sriram (1994) [2nd] shows convergence to the branching diffusion with immigration (the CIR/Feller diffusion) and the CLSE has a non-normal limit at normalising rate n; Ispány et al. [AB below] with offspring variance → 0 get an OU limit and rate n^{3/2}.
- [AB] **Ispány–Pap–van Zuijlen 2005**: nearly critical, offspring variance → 0: OU fluctuation limit; CLSE of the offspring mean asymptotically normal with norming n^{3/2}, against n^{1/2} (subcritical) and n (nearly critical, positive limiting variance).
- [AB] **Li 2011**: for nearly critical Jiřina processes with immigration and **non-zero initial values** the CLSE of the offspring mean is consistent but not asymptotically normal and the **CLSE of the immigration mean is not consistent**; the paper says this differs from the zero-initial-value case of Ispány et al. (Precedent for "the initial condition changes the answer", but for a moment estimator.)
- [AB] **Wei–Winnicki 1990**: the abstract only states the aim (a unified estimation procedure across sub-, critical, supercritical cases). **Herrera-Marín I's introduction attributes to Wei and Winnicki the inconsistency of an immigration-mean estimator in the critical case; I could not verify this.** A search-result summary of a thesis says the opposite (λ-estimator consistent except in the supercritical case) [SS], and the continuous-time critical CIR result above (Cor 2.3(i)) gives a consistent √log T rate for the baseline. Treat the attribution as unresolved and do not repeat it.
- [FT] **Anne–Lu–Yu–Zhou 2026** (closest recent neighbour; no Hawkes mention, 0 hits): discrete-time affine (compound autoregressive) processes, α_n = 1 + γ/n. Thm 1: the rescaled process converges to a CIR diffusion; the text recalls the Feller condition 2µ ≥ σ² for positivity of the limit. Thm 3: in the local-to-unity regime the OLS slope estimator is not n-consistent in the usual way and **the OLS intercept estimator is inconsistent**; they note the rates are slower than in INAR(1) and AR(1) because the state-dependent variance reduces the information. The scope is OLS only: no likelihood, no Fisher information, no lower bound, and the Feller condition is not used to separate estimable from non-estimable regimes (as far as I read).

All discrete-time estimation results I located concern moment estimators (CLS/OLS). I found none that computes likelihood information or proves an impossibility for the immigration mean in the nearly critical regime.

## 4. Regret / redundancy lower bounds

- No minimax regret or redundancy lower bound specific to Hawkes or point processes was found (two searches).
- [AB] **Rissanen 1984** (IEEE IT 30): sharpens and generalises the lower bound on universal code length "relative to the considered class of processes", links it to prediction and estimation, and shows one cannot predict Gaussian ARMA processes below a bound set by the information in the data. **Rissanen 1986** (Ann. Statist. 14): a fundamental theorem bounds code length, hence prediction error, below. Hypotheses (fixed-θ, almost-all-θ statements, dependence structure) are not in the abstracts and I did not obtain the full texts.
- [SS] Generic and adjacent: Abernethy et al. (COLT 2009) characterise minimax regret of online convex games by duality; Shamir–Szpankowski give maximal minimax regret (d/2) log T + (d/2) log(π/2) for logistic regression; Drmota et al. report a different constant, (½ + α/4)d log T, with unbounded weights and regularisation, i.e. the leading constant depends on boundedness assumptions; Bilodeau–Foster–Roy treat log-loss for nonparametric classes.
- **Reading.** The classical lower bounds I could identify are for a fixed parameter as the sample size grows. A lower bound for a triangular array in which ε = 1 − n tends to 0 with the horizon, with explicit ε-dependence, is not something I found. This is absence of evidence from a limited search, not evidence of absence (Merhav–Feder 1998, the standard survey, could not be read).

## 5. Solo

- The paper is **Piggott and Solo**, ICASSP 2018, pp. 6333–6337 (the sheet listed only "Solo"). [AB] A fixed-gain adaptive (online) distributed estimator for a Hawkes network; intensity modelled with a causal **Laguerre basis** expansion; the recursive structure of the basis gives a new two-time-scale algorithm based on **exponentially weighted least squares** that preserves non-negativity; simulations illustrate the results.
- Relevance: online, recursive, constant step ("fixed gain"): this overlaps the tracking contribution C5 conceptually. Differences visible from the abstract: least squares instead of likelihood/Fisher scoring; a common-pole Laguerre basis rather than a dictionary of distinct poles; simulation support stated, no regret bound, near-critical analysis or lower bound mentioned. I have not read the paper, so the "High" overlap rating for C5 should stand until the full text is read.
- Also seen: Rong and Nair (arXiv 2603.14942v3, March–May 2026) [AB], a batch constrained least-squares / SDP Hawkes identification with a Laguerre basis and a positivity-and-stability guarantee; not online, no regret; it is a different paper from the Rong–Nair entry already on the sheet (2602.20795).

## 6. Consequences for the project (hedged to what was verified)

1. **The bounded/log/divergent structure of the baseline information is classical in the CIR limit experiment** (Thm 2.1, Cor 2.3(i), Dehtiar Prop 1). Our K=1 Fisher matrix and cold-start coefficient agree with it numerically as ε → 0, which is independent support for Problem 10. What remains specific: the rigorous Hawkes prelimit (Theorem A and the rest), sums of exponentials (K ≥ 2), the explicit μ–n ridge, and the impossibility results for the stationary record (K=1: `theoremA_step2_and_stationary_tv.md`; any K: `general_K_impossibility.md`).
2. **The conjectured cold-start coefficient A = 2/(β(2μ−β))** in `problem10_rigorous.md` §4 equals the CIR critical-case information rate in Cor 2.3(i). The conjecture is supported in the limit experiment; the Hawkes prelimit transfer is still unproved.
3. **Overlap risk to add:** Anne–Lu–Yu–Zhou 2026 states, for local-to-unity discrete affine processes, that the intercept (baseline) estimator is inconsistent, with a CIR limit and a Feller remark. A reviewer may cite it against the *message* of C4 (my rating: Medium). The differences: OLS only, discrete time, no Hawkes, no information bound.
4. **Corrections to earlier notes:** (a) "Solo" is Piggott and Solo; (b) the title for sheet entry #16 is now confirmed; (c) Herrera-Marín I's attribution to Wei–Winnicki is unverified and conflicts with other sources; (d) my statement in `novelty_reassessment.md` that the dichotomy in the CIR limit experiment "may be fully classical" is now confirmed for fixed regimes, and not found for the local-to-critical one.
5. **Streaming/regret remains the part with no located prior art** beyond Yang et al. (regularised, O(log T)), Hall–Willett (tracking) and Piggott–Solo (EW least squares, simulations). A regret lower bound with explicit ε-dependence is not available from what I found.
